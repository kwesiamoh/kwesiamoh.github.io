PROJECT 01 · PROCESS ENGINEERING & EMISSIONS MONITORING
Virtual Soft-Sensor for Turbine Emissions Monitoring
Physics-informed neural network · Uncertainty-aware emissions prediction
GitHub ↗
Engineering Problem

Exhaust probes tracking CO and NOx emissions operate under harsh thermal conditions above 600°C. Sensor drift and hardware degradation can create gaps in monitoring data. During transient plant operations, these gaps carry real environmental compliance risks and can obscure how the process is actually performing.

Approach

Developed a deep-learning soft sensor with a thermodynamic loss penalty alongside the standard data error term:

ℒ = ℒdata + λ · ℒphysics

The formulation encourages predictions that remain consistent with thermodynamic relationships alongside the observed data. Monte Carlo Dropout is used to estimate predictive uncertainty during inference.

Results

The model estimates CO and NOx emissions from existing operational telemetry and presents predictions through an interactive monitoring dashboard. The workflow demonstrates how physics-informed soft sensing and uncertainty estimation can support engineering interpretation when conventional measurements are unavailable or unreliable.

PROJECT 02 · ENERGY SYSTEMS & DECARBONIZATION
German Energy Grid & Carbon Intensity Forecasting
Multi-horizon predictive pipeline · Benchmarking XGBoost vs. foundation models
GitHub ↗
Engineering Problem

Grid operators and industrial energy users need reliable short-term demand and carbon-intensity forecasts to identify periods of lower-carbon electricity availability. Recursive forecasting methods can accumulate error over longer horizons, reducing their usefulness for operational planning.

Approach

Built an end-to-end forecasting pipeline using approximately 80,000 hourly observations from German power system, weather, and market datasets. Engineered physically informed forecasting features and compared recursive XGBoost models with zero-shot Amazon Chronos-T5 across multiple prediction horizons.

Results

Compared with the recursive XGBoost baseline, Chronos-T5 maintained more stable forecasts across longer prediction horizons on the evaluated dataset. The workflow demonstrates an engineering-oriented approach to forecasting applications including industrial load scheduling and carbon-aware electricity use.

PROJECT 03 · CFD & PROCESS SIMULATION
OpenFOAM Projects
Multiphase, reacting, and biomedical flows · Independent meshing & solver configuration
GitHub ↗
Engineering Problem

Engineering CFD problems frequently involve multiphase, reacting, or non-Newtonian flows. Cases such as Rayleigh–Taylor instability, pulsatile aortic blood flow, and turbulent methane combustion require careful numerical setup to obtain stable and physically meaningful solutions.

Approach

Configured three independent OpenFOAM simulation workflows covering multiphase, reacting, and biomedical flows. Each case included geometry preparation, mesh generation, boundary-condition specification, solver configuration, transient simulation, and post-processing using ParaView.

Results

The projects demonstrate applied CFD capability across flow regimes relevant to process and energy engineering, including combustion, multiphase transport, and non-Newtonian fluid mechanics. The work emphasizes reproducible simulation workflows and physical interpretation rather than isolated numerical outputs.
