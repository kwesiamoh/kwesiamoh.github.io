PROJECT 01 · ENERGY SYSTEMS & DECARBONIZATION
German Energy Grid & Carbon Intensity Forecasting
Multi-horizon predictive pipeline · Benchmarking XGBoost vs. Foundation Models
GitHub ↗
Challenge

Grid operators need reliable 24-hour demand and carbon intensity forecasts to shift industrial loads toward renewable generation peaks. Standard autoregressive models compound errors over longer horizons, making it difficult to schedule loads effectively or reduce reliance on backup reserve capacity.

Solution

Built an automated pipeline ingesting 80,000 records from federal energy APIs. Physical composite features were engineered to capture diurnal cycles and grid load patterns. I ran a structured comparison between optimized XGBoost configurations and zero-shot deployment of Amazon Chronos-T5 across multiple forecast horizons.

Impact

Foundation models maintained predictive accuracy well beyond the 18-hour mark where classical models typically degrade. The results are relevant to industrial energy scheduling and carbon accounting, where forecast reliability directly affects operational planning. The pipeline is modular and adaptable to other grid zones.

PROJECT 02 · PROCESS ENGINEERING & EMISSIONS MONITORING
Virtual Soft-Sensor for Turbine Emissions Monitoring
Physics-informed neural network architecture · Uncertainty quantification for emissions inference
GitHub ↗
Challenge

Exhaust probes tracking CO and NOx emissions operate under harsh thermal conditions above 600°C. Sensor drift and hardware degradation create gaps in monitoring data. During transient plant operations, these gaps carry real environmental compliance risks and can obscure how the process is actually performing.

Solution

Developed a deep learning soft-sensor with a thermodynamic loss penalty alongside the standard data error term:

ℒ = ℒdata + λ · ℒphysics

This formulation keeps predictions consistent with mass and energy balances. Monte Carlo Dropout provides statistical confidence bounds at inference time, giving operators a sense of prediction reliability alongside each estimate.

Impact

The model infers emissions values from existing operational telemetry, reducing immediate dependency on auxiliary instrumentation. Results feed into an interactive dashboard for monitoring process health. The approach demonstrates physics-constrained emissions inference as a practical complement to physical sensor networks.

PROJECT 03 · CFD & PROCESS SIMULATION
OpenFOAM Projects
Multiphase, reacting, and biomedical flows · Independent meshing & solver configuration
GitHub ↗
Challenge

Industrial fluid dynamics problems rarely involve simple single-phase flows. Modeling phenomena like a Rayleigh–Taylor instability (water/oil mixing), non-Newtonian pulsatile aortic blood flow, or turbulent methane combustion requires careful numerical setup to maintain physical accuracy and prevent solver divergence.

Solution

Built three end-to-end simulation cases from first principles using OpenFOAM. Each workflow covers custom mesh generation, discretization scheme selection, runtime parameter tuning, and post-processing to extract velocity fields, species concentrations, and phase interface behavior.

Impact

Demonstrates applied CFD capability across flow regimes relevant to process and energy engineering: thermal mixing, reactive flows, and multiphase dynamics. Each case was built independently from geometry to results, without relying on pre-configured tutorials.
